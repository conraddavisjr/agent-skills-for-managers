The problem

Eleven months happen. Review week remembers three.

Recency bias isn't a character flaw, it's how memory works. Without a record, the person who shipped something quietly in March is competing with whoever was visible last week.

Capture

Four prompts instead of a blank page.

Evidence of what you saw, a Diagnosis of why it matters, the Immediate next step, and something Trackable so you know whether it changed. Trackable is the one most feedback skips — it's the difference between a note and a commitment.

Filing

Log it from their page. It files itself.

There's only one table — every observation about everyone lives in it. Each person's year page is a filtered window onto that table, so hitting New there tags the note with the right person before you've typed a word.

Patterns

See where your attention actually went.

Once the notes exist, they add up to something you can't get any other way: a picture of your own management. The empty slice is the useful one — it's usually a blind spot rather than a team with nothing to say on the subject.

The payoff

Review season becomes reading, not remembering.

You open the year and it's already written. The Exemplary rows are the promotion case; the Improve rows are the development plan. Nobody's contribution depends on whether it happened close enough to December for you to recall it.
